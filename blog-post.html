How often should youreally see a dentist?

Many people only visit a dentist when something hurts. But by then, a small problem has often become a bigger and more expensive one. So how often should you really go?

The short answer for most people is every six months. The right gap for you depends on your teeth, your gums and your habits. Here's how to work it out.

Why it matters

Tooth decay and gum disease usually start silently. A small cavity doesn't hurt, and early gum disease may show up only as a little bleeding when you brush. At a check-up, we can spot these early and fix them with a simple filling or a cleaning, instead of a root canal or an extraction later.

“The cheapest and most comfortable treatment is the one you never need, and regular check-ups make that possible.”— Dentify Dental Team

Regular cleanings also remove hardened tartar that no toothbrush can shift, along with stains from tea, coffee and paan. Your teeth feel smoother and your breath stays fresher.

How often to visit

As a general guide:

  • Every 6 months: most adults and children with healthy teeth and gums.
  • Every 3–4 months: people with gum disease, diabetes, braces or aligners, smokers, and pregnant women.
  • Up to once a year: some adults with very low risk, if your dentist agrees.

Signs to book sooner

Don't wait for your next check-up if you notice any of these:

  1. Toothache, or sensitivity to hot or cold that lingers
  2. Gums that bleed, swell or look like they're pulling back
  3. A broken tooth or filling, a loose tooth, or bad breath that won't go away
Good to know: Children should have their first dental visit around their first birthday. Early visits are short, gentle and help them feel at home in the dental chair.

The takeaway
